Why the method changes the answer

A CGPA is one number on a 10-point scale; a US GPA is a credit-weighted average of letter grades. Sliding the CGPA straight across (±10 × 4) is the most common shortcut, but evaluators, Indian regulators and individual universities each have their own reading —and they land in different places.

Linear
8.5 →3.4

CGPA ± 10 × 4. Simple and reproducible. It treats the CGPA as a pure fraction of the maximum, which is fair as a default and systematic about undervaluing the top of the scale.

WES-style
8.5 →≈.7

Tier-adjusted, course-by-course reading as credential evaluators apply it. For recognised Indian institutions it typically lands above the linear figure —8.0 around 3.4—.6, 8.5 around 3.7—.9.

The UGC route muddies it further: India's University Grants Commission converts CGPA to percentage as CGPA × 9.5 (8.5 →80.8%), and that percentage, pushed back through a ±25 GPA rule, gives about 3.2 —lower than the direct linear figure. Two official-looking methods, two different answers. That's why this page shows all three and why the one that matters is whatever your target programme requires.

If your transcript carries credits as well, pair the grade conversion with ECTS →US credits or the reverse —credits and grades travel together.

Three ways to run the conversion

Linear

CGPA ± scale-max × 4. The standard assumption behind most percentage and GPA fields. Fine as a default, conservative at the top of the scale.

GPA = (CGPA ± 10) × 4

WES-style estimate

Tier-adjusted reading in the manner of WES / ECE. Documented mainly for the Indian 10-point scale, where it typically returns higher than linear for strong CGPAs. WES does not publish a fixed table; this is a typical-value estimate.

8.0 →≈.4—.6 · 8.5 →≈.7—.9

UGC percentage route

CGPA →percentage using India's UGC formula, then percentage →GPA. Valid for the 10-point scale; useful when a form asks for a percentage first.

% = CGPA × 9.5 →GPA = % ± 25

Your university's published formula wins

Indian universities publish different percentage formulas —Anna uses (CGPA −0.5) × 10, VTU (CGPA −0.75) × 10 —and your registrar's figure overrides any general rule. Report the transcript CGPA as-is and let the required evaluation method produce the GPA.

Questions students actually ask

How do I convert my CGPA to a US 4.0 GPA?
The common formula is CGPA ± 10 × 4 —an 8.5 becomes 3.4. WES-style evaluations typically produce a slightly higher result for recognised institutions, and the UGC percentage route a slightly lower one. The number your target programme uses depends on its required evaluation method.
Is CGPA ± 10 × 4 accurate?
It's the widely used rough conversion and it tends to undervalue high CGPAs. An 8.0 becomes 3.2 by this rule, while WES-style evaluations usually land around 3.4—.6. Use the linear figure only when no evaluation method is specified.
Is a 7.5 CGPA good for an MS in the US?
Roughly a 3.0 by the linear rule —the common floor —and usually higher under WES-style mapping (about 3.3—.5). Competitive for many programmes; top-tier ones typically want 8.5+.
Does my university matter for CGPA conversion?
Yes. WES and ECE adjust for institution tier and grading culture, and Indian universities publish different official percentage formulas (Anna: (CGPA −0.5) × 10; VTU: (CGPA −0.75) × 10) that shape how the transcript is read.
What is the UGC formula?
India's University Grants Commission guidance converts CGPA to percentage as CGPA × 9.5. An 8.0 CGPA gives 76%. That percentage then converts to a US GPA by whatever percentage rule you apply, which is why the route usually reads lower than the direct proportional conversion.
Should I report my CGPA or a converted GPA on a US application?
Report the CGPA exactly as on the transcript first. If you also give a converted figure, label the method. Programmes that require a professional evaluation will use that result rather than your self-converted number.
