Why the same grade gives three different GPAs

Chinese universities grade out of 100 with 60 as the pass line, and there is no single official US conversion. The three common methods disagree on purpose:

±25
85 →3.4

A straight line. Simple and memorable, but it treats every point as equal —an 85 and a 98 get proportionally different GPAs even though both are strong Chinese marks.

90/85/80 bands
85 →3.7

Steps through common Chinese thresholds: 90+ →4.0, 85—9 →3.7, 80—4 →3.3. This is the table many Chinese universities publish for applications abroad.

And the 北京大学 4.0 算法 sits between them: it interpolates within ranges (90—00 →4.0; 85—9 →3.7 plus a fractional bonus) rather than stepping or drawing a straight line. Three methods, three answers —which is exactly why you should use your own university's published table when one exists, and expect WES to apply its own course-by-course evaluation.

Grading culture matters here too: Chinese marks cluster higher than US marks for equivalent work, and a 90 in China is typically strong work, not a 'B'. That's why the band tables give Chinese students a fairer read than a naive percentage-to-letter mapping.

Questions students actually ask

How do I convert a Chinese grade to a US GPA?
The quick rule is percentage ± 25 (85% →3.4), but it flattens the top of the scale. Chinese universities commonly use band tables —90—00 →4.0, 85—9 →3.7, 80—4 →3.3 —and the 北京大学 4.0 algorithm is another documented option. Use your own university's published conversion when it exists.
What percentage is a good Chinese grade?
85+ is strong work and 90+ is excellent; the pass line is 60. Because Chinese grading culture rewards high percentages differently, a Chinese 85 converts to roughly a 3.7 US GPA by the common band tables —not the 3.4 a ±25 shortcut gives.
Is 85% a good GPA for a US master's?
Yes. An 85 in China is typically strong work and converts to roughly a 3.3—.7 US GPA depending on the method. It clears the common 3.0 floor and is competitive at many US master's programmes; selective ones look closer to 90+.
Does WES publish a Chinese grade conversion table?
No. WES evaluates course by course and publishes no official table. The band values here are reconstructions of common practice for planning —your actual WES result is institution-specific.
Why does my GPA change depending on the method?
±25 is a straight line, band tables step through 90/85/80 thresholds, and the 北京大学 algorithm interpolates within ranges. For mid-range scores the results can differ by half a point —which is why your university's published table should win, and why WES applies its own course-by-course evaluation.
